How to play

Yutdugi

The Korean stick game you win by thinking

Play now

A classic, without the luck

For centuries Koreans have played a board game with four wooden sticks: you throw them, count how many land flat side up, and race your pieces around the board. It is officially called Yunnori and has been a National Intangible Cultural Heritage of Korea since 2022.

Yutdugi keeps the board and the sticks but takes away the throw. Every stick is in plain sight and you change the result yourself, one flip at a time. Like chess or Go, it is a game of perfect information: whoever reads further ahead wins.

1The sticks

Each stick has two sides. Only the flat side counts.

There are four sticks in play. Each player holds two, and the result is always read from all four together.

2Reading the result

Count the flat sides showing. The five results carry the animal names of the traditional game — pig, dog, sheep, cow and horse — and tell you how far to move.

3Your turn: flip exactly one

On your turn you must flip one of your own two sticks — never none, never both, never your opponent’s. The flip changes the count by one, so the result moves one step along this ladder:

Horse↔Pig↔Dog↔Sheep↔Cow

In the example above it is Blue’s turn, with Sheep showing. Blue has two choices:

Flip the round stick upCow · 4four flat sides
Flip the flat stick downDog · 2two flat sides

Then you move one of your pieces that many spaces. Every flip also changes what your opponent can do next — that is where the thinking comes in.

The first two moves are fixed. All sticks start round side up, so move 1 is always Pig and move 2 is always Dog. The real game starts with move 3.

4Moving around the board

Outer pathShortcut from Corner AShortcut from Corner B
  • Each player has four pieces. They enter at Start (bottom right) and travel up the right side, around the board.
  • Shortcuts: if a piece stops exactly on Corner A, Corner B or the Center, its next move cuts across the diagonal. Pass over the spot without stopping and you stay on the outer path. From the Center, the shortcut heads straight for home.
  • Capture: land on a space holding your opponent’s piece and it goes back off the board.
  • Stack: land on your own piece and they move together from then on (and can be captured together).
  • Getting home: stopping exactly on Start is not enough — a piece must move past it. Bring all four pieces home to win.

5Two rules that keep it fair

No extra turns. In the traditional game a capture, a Cow or a Horse earns another throw. Not here — play always passes to the other player.

No repeats. You may not make a move that creates the same position (pieces, sticks and whose turn it is) for the third time. It works like ko in Go and stops endless back-and-forth. The game is a draw only if every legal move would break this rule; the game marks any move it blocks.

6Optional: the fair-start rule

Moving first can be an advantage. With the fair-start rule on, the first player makes move 3 — the first real choice — and then the second player decides which side to take. A move that is too strong gets taken away and a weak one gets kept, so the first player has to aim for a balanced position.

Quick reference

  1. Flip exactly one of your own sticks.
  2. Count flat sides across all four: Pig 1 · Dog 2 · Sheep 3 · Cow 4 · none = Horse 5.
  3. Move one piece that far. Capture, stack, take shortcuts.
  4. No extra turns. No third repeat of a position.
  5. First to bring all four pieces past Start wins.
